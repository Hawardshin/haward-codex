# Positive Vision Agent Research Note

## Conclusion

`positive-vision-agent` should not merely say "you can do it." Its job is to structure why the work is worth doing, what can be controlled, which paths are plausible, and how to adapt when blocked.

## Evidence Summary

- Hope Theory treats `agency` and `pathways` as core components of goal pursuit, so positive vision should include both will and alternative routes.
- Implementation-intentions research suggests that goals need situation-specific `if-then` plans to improve follow-through.
- Goal-setting theory supports specific, challenging goals, but the effect depends on feedback, commitment, ability, and task complexity.
- Psychological-safety research warns that positive climate must not suppress problem reporting or dissent.

## Agent Design Principles

- north star: state the desired future clearly.
- agency: identify what can be controlled now.
- pathways: provide at least two plausible paths.
- if-then: create obstacle-specific implementation intentions.
- reality check: disclose risks, unknowns, and verification gates.

## Example Uses

- "This is too big" becomes "the desired end state is this, today's first experiment is this, and blocked paths can switch to A or B."
- "There is no time" pairs `timekeeper-agent` timeboxing with scope trimming and a reachable first outcome.
- "I am not sure" becomes testable hypotheses and fallback paths.
